### Problem Addressed and Problem Importance
Existing studies of AI agents focus on technical exposure or usage data but rarely capture worker desires for automation versus augmentation or the preferred level of human involvement. This leaves a gap in understanding mismatches between what workers want and what technology can deliver.

### State of Related Works in This Topic
Prior exposure research (Eloundou et al., Anthropic Economic Index) measured theoretical or observed coverage but did not incorporate worker preferences or a nuanced agency scale. Audits were often narrow (software engineering, customer support) or capital-centric.

### Proposed Solution
The authors introduce a worker-centric auditing framework using audio-enhanced mini-interviews and the Human Agency Scale (H1–H5) to quantify desired human involvement. They construct WORKBank, a database of 1,500 worker responses and 52 AI-expert assessments across 844 tasks in 104 occupations. Tasks are divided into four zones based on desire and capability: Automation “Green Light,” Automation “Red Light,” R&D Opportunity, and Low Priority. They find workers want automation mainly for low-value repetitive tasks, reveal mismatches in current investments, and document a shift in valued skills from information processing to interpersonal competence.

### Drawbacks and Limitations
The sample covers only computer-compatible tasks and relies on self-reported desires that may evolve with experience. Expert capability assessments are forward-looking but still subjective.

### Future Research
Expanding WORKBank with more occupations and longitudinal data, testing how actual agent deployments shift worker preferences, and using the Human Agency Scale to guide responsible agent design and policy.
