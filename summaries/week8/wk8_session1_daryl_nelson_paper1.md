Chain-of-Thought Prompting Elicits Reasoning in Large Language Models

This paper explores a simple but powerful idea in the world of large language models: if you ask these models to show their thinking steps, called chain-of-thought prompting, they can actually solve hard reasoning problems much better than before. The authors wanted to understand how giving a few examples where an answer is broken down into intermediate reasoning steps can help big models perform things like arithmetic, commonsense, and symbolic reasoning tasks. They tested this on three big language models, including a 540-billion parameter model, and found that just a few examples with chains of thought made the models much more accurate on benchmarks like GSM8K math problems, even beating models that were fine-tuned for those tasks.

Their methodology was mostly experimental: they set up few-shot prompts with and without reasoning steps and compared performance across different tasks. Their results were clear that chain-of-thought prompting boosts performance significantly, which means that the way you phrase your prompt really matters when you want reasoning from these models.

From my understanding, the paper shows that large models don't just memorize patterns but can reason more if guided with examples of reasoning. A strength is how broad the evaluation is across tasks, but a weakness might be that it still needs human-written exemplars, which can be costly to create. Overall the goals were achieved and its relevance is high for anyone working with prompting and reasoning in language models.
