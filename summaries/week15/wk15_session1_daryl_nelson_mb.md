The paper Future of Work with AI Agents: Auditing Automation and Augmentation Potential across the U.S. Workforce looks at how AI agents may actually change jobs across the workforce. The main reason for this research is that most discussions about AI focus too much on full automation, while not enough attention is given to how workers interact with AI or prefer to use it. The authors try to address this gap by creating a more detailed and human centered evaluation.

In terms of methodology, the paper combines worker surveys with expert assessment of AI capabilities. They collect data from around 1500 workers across many occupations and build a structured dataset called WORKBank, which includes hundreds of tasks. They also introduce a Human Agency Scale to measure how much control workers want to keep versus delegate to AI. This approach is quite interesting because it mixes both technical and human perspectives.

The results show that most tasks are not fully automatable. Instead, many fall into a middle category where AI can assist but not replace humans. The study finds that augmentation is more common than full automation, and that workers often prefer to maintain some level of control. This suggests that the future of work will involve collaboration rather than replacement.

I think the goals of the research were mostly accomplished; since it provides clearer and more realistic picture of AI in the workplace. My understanding is that the paper argues for a balanced view where AI reshapes tasks instead of just eliminating them. A strength is the combination of real worker input and systematic analysis, a weakness is that the results depend on current AI abilities which may change over time.
